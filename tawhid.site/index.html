Tawhid: The Oneness of God

The foundation of Islamic belief: affirming that God alone deserves to be worshipped, and that He has no partner in His dominion, His actions, or His attributes.

What Is Tawhid?

Tawhid is singling out God, glorified and exalted is He, in what is unique to Him: His Lordship, His Divinity, and His Names and Attributes.

Linguistically: Tawhid comes from the verb wahhada, meaning to make something one and not many, or to set something apart from everything else.

In Islamic terminology: It is the firm belief that God is One in His essence, His attributes, and His actions; that He has no partner in His kingdom or in His management of the universe; and that He alone is worthy of worship.

The Three Categories of Tawhid

Scholars divided Tawhid into three main categories to make its meaning clear and to show how it is applied.

1

Tawhid ar-Rububiyyah

توحيد الربوبية

Oneness of Lordship. Singling out God in His own actions, such as creating, providing, giving life, causing death, and managing the affairs of the universe.

Example: A Muslim believes that there is truly no creator, provider, or controller of the universe except God alone.

2

Tawhid al-Uluhiyyah

توحيد الألوهية

Oneness of Worship. Singling out God in every kind of worship, both outward and inward, such as supplication, fear, hope, reliance, prayer, and sacrifice.

Example: None of these acts of worship is directed to anyone other than God. This is the true meaning of La ilaha illa Allah: there is no one worthy of worship except God.

3

Tawhid al-Asma’ wa as-Sifat

توحيد الأسماء والصفات

Oneness of Names and Attributes. Believing in everything God has affirmed about Himself in His Book, or through His Messenger, peace be upon him, of the most beautiful names and the most exalted attributes, in a manner that suits His majesty.

This is done without distorting their meaning, without denying them, without asking “how,” and without comparing God to His creation.

The Statement of Faith (Shahada)

Entering Islam and holding to Tawhid is expressed in a two-part testimony:

لا إله إلا الله، محمد رسول الله

La ilaha illa Allah, Muhammad Rasul Allah

There is no god but God, and Muhammad is the Messenger of God.

The first part, La ilaha illa Allah, rests on two essential pillars:

1. Negation

لا إله

Rejecting and uprooting worship and divinity from everything other than God. This is disbelief in taghut, everything worshipped besides God.

2. Affirmation

إلا الله

Affirming worship and divinity for God alone, who has no partner. This is belief in God.

The second part, Muhammad Rasul Allah, means believing that Muhammad is God’s servant and final Messenger, and that God’s message reached us through him.

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

Muslims believe Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, is the last of God’s prophets and messengers. He was sent with the same core message as the prophets before him: worship God alone.

محمد بن عبد الله ﷺ

Muhammad, son of Abdullah

  • Birth in Mecca (about 570 CE). He was born into the tribe of Quraysh and lost his father before birth and his mother in early childhood. He was raised by his grandfather and then his uncle.
  • Known as “al-Amin.” Even before prophethood, people called him the Trustworthy because of his honesty and good character.
  • The first revelation (about 610 CE). At the age of forty, the angel Gabriel came to him in the Cave of Hira with the first verses of the Qur’an.
  • Calling to Tawhid. He invited people to worship God alone and to leave the worship of idols, and he faced years of hardship and persecution for it.
  • The Hijrah (622 CE). He migrated from Mecca to Medina, where the first Muslim community was established. This event marks the start of the Islamic calendar.
  • Passing away (632 CE). He passed away in Medina after conveying the message, and was buried there.

What does it mean to testify that he is God’s Messenger?

To obey him in what he commanded, to believe what he told us, to stay away from what he forbade, and to worship God only in the ways he taught.
